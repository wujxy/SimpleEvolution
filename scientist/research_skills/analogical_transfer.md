# Transfer a mechanism from a distant domain

Use this method when the current framing has stopped yielding — the
remaining moves inside it are all refinements — and you want to see the
problem differently before deciding what to ask next.

Strip the problem to its structure: name the objects, the relations
between them, and the constraints that make it hard. Remove the domain
nouns. State the structural tension in one sentence.

Find two or three problems from distant fields that share that structure.
A Searcher engagement can carry the search; ask for problems shaped like
the abstraction, not for solutions to your own field's version. Distant
beats near — a neighbouring domain imports its own clichés.

For each analogue, ask how that field resolves the structural tension —
not its vocabulary, the mechanism. Map the mechanism back into concrete
terms here: what it would imply, what it would change, what it predicts
that the current framing does not.

Then mark the map's failure points honestly: where the analogy is only
surface, where the domains genuinely differ, where the transferred
mechanism would break. A mapped mechanism that survives its disanalogy
list is worth keeping; one that does not is a discarded lens, not a
result.

The outcome is a new way of seeing the problem — a different research
question, a mechanism not previously considered, a competing explanation,
or a discriminating experiment the current framing would not have
produced. A concrete solution may fall out, but that is not the purpose;
do not force the method to end in an implementation.
