# assumption_audit — finding the load-bearing defaults

Load this when a plan is formed and about to consume resources, or
when a complex problem refuses to yield. Research rarely stalls for
lack of ideas; it stalls on an unexamined default. The load-bearing
assumptions are the ones whose falsehood would change the plan —
everything else can wait.

## The audit

List the plan's load-bearing assumptions. The test for load-bearing:
if this were false, would the plan change materially? Include the
inherited ones — what arrived in the goal's framing, a predecessor's
memo, a colleague's report — at full weight, for nobody audits what
everyone repeats.

Rank by impact if false, times cheapness of checking — not by
confidence. Confidence is where walls hide: "everyone assumes" is a
fact about the team, not about the world.

Give each assumption a falsifier: the specific observation that would
refute it. An assumption with no imaginable falsifier is not an
assumption — it is a definition, and definitions are free; move it
out of the ledger.

Check the dangerous-cheap ones first, before committing resources to
any leg of the plan. The canonical case: a constraint inherited as
"bit-exact" whose actual text says "within 1e-13" — one read of the
original document, and a wall that shaped an entire campaign
disappears.

## The ledger

Keep the list for the life of the plan. When the plan fails, the
ledger says which assumption to blame — a failure attributed to
nothing teaches nothing, and a dead assumption is banked knowledge
(the research memory is where it lives).

Provenance: Co-Scientist's iterative decomposition; the r6 inherited
"bit-exact" default.
