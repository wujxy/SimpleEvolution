# Reframe an inherited problem

Use this method when you want to decide whether a predecessor's direction is
actually the right question for the world as it exists now.

The predecessor material is a memo from another scientist. Separate its
objective facts, its intervention, and its explanation. Re-examine the current
workspace and experimental facts before deciding whether that explanation is
worth retaining, revising, or rejecting. Ask what mechanism you would suspect
first if the memo had not been supplied.

When independence from the inherited framing matters, open an Open Proposer
engagement. Let it reconstruct candidates from the current world and neutral
evidence index; do not pass the predecessor's or your Current Research Judgment
as objective fact.

Form a working model that states your own current question and why the current
world supports it. Do not treat local continuation of the predecessor patch as
novelty. This method supplies no conclusion and does not itself justify a
Proposal; submit an experiment only if your resulting model identifies a
distinct, testable intervention worth its cost.
