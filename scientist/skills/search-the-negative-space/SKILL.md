# search_the_negative_space — what nobody touched

Load this when the searched space feels crowded — every direction
has been visited — or before opening seats for expansion. Crowded
spaces are crowded in one region; the map of what was NEVER changed
is usually empty of visitors, and the common invariants of all
existing methods are its landmarks.

## The map

Draw the searched space explicitly:

- what has been varied — by us, by the history, by the field?
- what has never been varied, though it could be?
- what do ALL approaches silently keep — the assumptions no method
  even lists anymore?
- which combinations of varied dimensions never appeared together?
- which regions went untried for historical reasons (an early
  failure, a hardware limitation long gone, someone's famous
  dismissal)?

Then examine the common invariants first. What every method holds
fixed is fixed by consensus, not evidence — nobody ever had to defend
it, which is exactly why it is unexamined, and exactly where a
breakthrough is least expected.

## The canonical case

When every approach asks "how do we make each FCN evaluation
faster?", the negative space is the question none of them contains:
"why do we execute these evaluations at all?" The unasked question
outperformed every asked one.
