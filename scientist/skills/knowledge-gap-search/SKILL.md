# knowledge_gap_search — decomposing the unknown

Load this when judgment is blocked on what you don't know, or when
one query keeps returning one kind of answer. "I don't understand X"
is not one gap — it is a bundle, and searching the bundle returns one
kind of answer every time. Decomposed, it returns answers.

## Decompose first, open second

Before opening any seat, split the vague unknown into distinct
questions, each answerable on its own:

- the mechanism — how does X actually work?
- the field standard — how do practitioners of this domain handle X?
- prior art — who has already solved this, and where is the record?
- failure cases — where has this failed, and with what signature?
- definitions — what exactly does this parameter/concept denote here?
- reusable assets — is there code, data, or a procedure to take
  outright?

One searcher per distinct question, each entering through its own
door: literature, the codebase, internal records, the data
dictionary. A question that can be answered from material already in
hand does not earn a seat.

## Prior art before invention

A verified path is cheaper than a novel one. Before investing in a
direction, check the places finished work accumulates: the
literature, the disk (a predecessor's implementation), the research
memory's dead lanes. Finding that the problem is solved is a result,
not an embarrassment.

## What comes back

Each question returns one decidable sentence — an answer that could
change a decision — not a bibliography. Stop when the blocking
judgment unblocks; knowledge gathered beyond the block is decoration.

A gap no search can close (it needs a measurement or an experiment)
hands off to experiment design, not to more searchers.

Provenance: searcher practice across r1-r7; the XSBench campaigns,
where one searcher sentence repeatedly decided a direction.
