# hypothesis_recombination — complementary structure, not average assembly

Load this when the table holds partial successes: directions that
worked somewhere and failed somewhere. Averaging them produces the
worst of both; recombination asks which parts were load-bearing in
each success.

## The recombinations

Take A and B, each partially validated, and construct:

- A's mechanism in B's representation
- A's observation with B's explanation
- A's algorithm with B's constraint handling
- A minus its unnecessary part (simplification is recombination with
  a null parent)
- A and B lifted to the one deeper mechanism they are both instances
  of
- the unified scheme that answers why A worked in region X while B
  worked in region Y — a scheme that explains the split is a new
  hypothesis, not a merge.

## The discipline

Keep lineage: every recombinant records its parents and the one part
that changed. Failures then localize to the part, and the next
recombination is informed rather than random.

The test of a real recombination is complementarity: the result
should be something neither parent could reach, not a midpoint
between them. If the recombinant is exactly as good as the better
parent, nothing was combined.

Provenance: Co-Scientist's combination and simplification operators;
the r1-r7 candidate pools, where the surviving wins were mechanisms
from one lane carried by another lane's structure.
